Insurance · Life

Life insurance: term, whole, and universal

How it works, why it matters, and how to find coverage that fits your household — explained plainly, without the jargon.

A life insurance policy is an agreement with an insurer: you pay premiums, and if you pass away while the policy is active, they pay your beneficiaries a tax-free benefit — often enough to cover years of living expenses your income would have provided.

Certain policy types, like whole and universal life, also build cash value over time with tax advantages. That makes life insurance both a safety net and, for some households, a long-term financial planning tool.

Who is it for?

Anyone with people who depend on their income or care — a partner, kids, aging parents, or a business.

What does it cover?

A death benefit paid to your beneficiaries, almost always as an income-tax-free lump sum.

Why consider it?

Beyond income replacement, it can fund final expenses, support a business transition, or build family assets.

The basics

What are the three main types of life insurance?

Term life

Affordable, temporary, no cash value

Coverage for a fixed period — often 10 to 30 years — at the lowest cost. When the term ends, coverage ends unless you renew at a higher rate.

Learn more →

Whole life

Lifelong coverage, fixed premiums

Permanent protection with guaranteed premiums and a death benefit that never expires, plus cash value you can borrow against.

Learn more →

Universal life

Lifelong coverage, flexible premiums

Permanent protection like whole life, but with the option to adjust your payments within a range as your circumstances change.

Learn more →
Choosing the right coverage

Start with what matters most to you

I need temporary coverage

If you only need protection until a mortgage is paid off or kids finish school, term life usually gives you the most coverage for the lowest cost.

I want to build assets

Whole and universal life build tax-advantaged cash value alongside lifelong protection — useful for estate planning or a family trust.

I want predictable costs

Whole life locks in your premium for life. Universal life trades some predictability for flexibility to adjust payments over time.

Cost

What affects your premium

Life insurance often costs less than people expect — a healthy 30-year-old can typically get substantial term coverage for the price of a streaming subscription. Your rate depends on a few key factors:

Age

Younger applicants generally get lower rates.

Health

Healthier applicants qualify for preferred pricing.

Smoking

Tobacco and vaping use tends to raise cost.

Medical history

Personal or family history can affect rates.

Lifestyle

High-risk hobbies may increase premiums.

Occupation

Higher-risk jobs can add to the cost.

Getting covered

Three ways to get life insurance

At work

Many employers offer group life coverage at lower rates with minimal underwriting — though it may not follow you if you change jobs.

Online

Term coverage is easy to quote and buy online in minutes. Permanent policies are usually better suited to personalized guidance.

Through an advisor

If you're unsure what fits, a financial professional can walk through your options and help you decide.

Frequently asked

At what age should I get life insurance?

Any age with dependents or financial obligations — the younger and healthier you are, the lower your rate tends to be.

Can I have more than one policy?

Yes. Many people combine employer group coverage with an individual policy to reach the amount they need.

Are payouts taxable?

Death benefits are almost always income-tax-free, and cash value growth in permanent policies is typically tax-deferred.

What if I have a health condition?

Many people with health conditions still qualify, sometimes through simplified or guaranteed-issue policies with fewer questions and no exam.

Insurance · Dental

Dental insurance

Coverage that helps keep everyday checkups and bigger procedures affordable, with access to a wide network of dentists.

Dental insurance helps cover the cost of oral care — from routine cleanings to fillings, crowns, and other procedures — so a trip to the dentist doesn't come with a surprise bill.

Who is it for?

Anyone who wants predictable costs for routine and unexpected dental care.

What does it cover?

Preventive visits, basic procedures like fillings, and major work such as crowns or root canals.

Why consider it?

Untreated dental issues can affect overall health — regular coverage encourages regular checkups.

Coverage levels

A typical dental plan covers care in three tiers

Preventive

Cleanings, exams, and X-rays — usually covered in full, twice a year.

Basic

Fillings, extractions, and similar procedures, typically covered partially.

Major

Crowns, bridges, and root canals — covered at a lower percentage, often after a waiting period.

Frequently asked

Is there a waiting period?

Many plans apply a waiting period for major procedures, though preventive care is usually covered right away.

Can I keep my current dentist?

If your dentist is in-network you'll typically pay less, but many plans allow out-of-network visits at a reduced benefit.

Is orthodontic care included?

Some plans offer optional orthodontic coverage, often with its own annual or lifetime maximum.

Insurance · Accident

Accident insurance

A lump-sum cash benefit after a covered accident — to spend on medical bills, everyday costs, or anything else that comes up.

Accident insurance pays you directly when a covered injury happens — separate from your health plan — so you can use the money wherever it's needed most, from copays to rent.

Who is it for?

Active households, families with kids in sports, or anyone wanting a cash cushion beyond health insurance.

What does it cover?

A fixed cash benefit tied to the type of injury and treatment received, paid straight to you.

Why consider it?

Health plans often leave gaps — deductibles, copays, time off work — this coverage helps fill them.

What's covered

Common covered events

Fractures & dislocations

Benefit amount varies by bone and severity.

ER visits

A flat benefit for emergency room treatment.

Hospital stays

A daily benefit for each night admitted.

Physical therapy

Per-visit benefit for follow-up rehab care.

Ambulance transport

A set benefit for ground or air transport.

Concussions

A benefit for diagnosed head injuries.

Frequently asked

Does this replace health insurance?

No — it's a supplement that pays cash alongside your existing health coverage.

How fast are claims paid?

Many accident claims are processed within days once documentation is submitted.

Can I use the payout however I want?

Yes — the benefit is paid to you directly with no restriction on how it's spent.
